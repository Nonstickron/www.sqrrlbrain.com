# Sqrrlbrain Studio — Social Posting Strategy

**Audience for this doc:** Ron. Solo operator, evening hours, editorial brand, allergic to becoming "an influencer."
**Date:** 2026-05-04
**Status:** v1 — opinionated starting point, expects to be edited as you actually start posting and learn what hits.

---

## 1 — Reality check before anything else

**The trap most solo studios fall into:** trying to be on every platform, posting every day, and burning out in 6 weeks with nothing to show. Then a year of silence. Then they try again with the same plan. This is the default failure mode.

**What actually works for solo operators:**
- One platform-format that covers multiple algorithms
- One capture system that doesn't require "shoot days"
- A weekly cadence you can hit even on a bad week
- Content rooted in what you're already doing, not staged for camera
- A 6-month minimum commitment before judging results

**What success looks like at month 6** (not month 1): ~20-30 published videos, a slowly growing baseline of viewers, a clear sense of which 2-3 video types perform best for you, and ZERO influencer-personality requirement. You're a studio that publishes a craft journal in video form, not a content creator.

---

## 2 — The format-first principle

You have four platforms on the table: TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook. **Don't pick a platform — pick a format.**

### Vertical short-form (60-90 seconds, 9:16)

One video, shot vertical, plays on:
- **TikTok** — strongest discovery algorithm for unknown accounts
- **Instagram Reels** — repurposes your existing IG presence
- **YouTube Shorts** — Google indexes them, evergreen reach
- **Facebook Reels** — bonus reach when you wire up Facebook

**One shoot. One edit. Four platforms.** This is the highest-leverage move you can make. Don't shoot landscape. Don't shoot 16:9. Vertical first, always.

### Long-form YouTube (8-15 minutes, 16:9)

Defer for now. Do this in **month 4-6** once you know which short-form videos perform best — those become the seeds for long-form deep-dives. Trying to do both formats in month 1 = burnout guaranteed.

### Why not Twitter/X, LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky?

Text-only platforms reward different muscles (writing voice, hot takes, networking). They're parallel work, not the same as video. Skip until short-form is humming.

## 3 — Three content pillars

A "pillar" is a recurring video type that visitors learn to expect from you. Three is the magic number — fewer feels one-note, more becomes random.

### Pillar 1 — Studio process

**What:** Hands-on-camera footage of you actually making something. Designing a dieline at DME. Generating a TRACT image. Building a meal-planner UI. Mixing a Crucible formulation. Sanding a shelf for the kids' room. Burning wood. Brewing.

**Why it works for you:** Print-template engineering, formulation chemistry, AI-driven image gen, and woodworking are all *visually rich*. Most viewers have never watched anyone do these things. Your day-job is content other people pay to learn about.

**Format hook:** "How a [X] gets made in [time]." Time-lapse the boring parts; real-time the satisfying moments (knife cuts, screen reveals, brush strokes).

**Cadence:** 2 of 4 weekly videos.

### Pillar 2 — Tool & product launches

**What:** Behind-the-scenes of a thing you're shipping. The day Inkwell goes live. A TRACT design dropping. A SqrrledAway feature reveal. A 5 Daughters product photo session. Even small ones — "I added a button today, here's why it matters."

**Why it works for you:** You're shipping constantly across multiple projects. Every shipped feature is a video. This pillar also doubles as launch promotion, so the marketing work is the content work.

**Format hook:** "I just shipped [X]. Here's what it does and why I built it." 60 seconds, end with where to find it.

**Cadence:** 1 of 4 weekly videos.

### Pillar 3 — Process journal / lessons

**What:** A reflective, shorter-form video about something you learned, hit a wall on, or solved. "I tried [thing], here's what worked." "This is why I stopped using [tool]." "How I plan a week of side-project work."

**Why it works for you:** You're *opinionated* and *experienced* (25 years at DME, self-taught, multiple shipped projects, confident technical takes). This pillar showcases that without requiring you to perform.

**Format hook:** Voiceover-only over screen recording, B-roll of your workspace, or a still of the thing you're talking about. No face needed.

**Cadence:** 1 of 4 weekly videos.

### What's *NOT* a pillar (yet)

- Personal life (transition, family, health) — only include if you choose to. Doesn't have to be a content category.
- Hot takes / industry commentary — burns trust quickly, and you don't need it.
- Trends / dance audio — you're not 22 and the editorial brand doesn't fit it.
- Day-in-the-life vlogs — high-effort, low-payoff for studio brands.

## 4 — Capture-first workflow

The single biggest unlock: **stop thinking about "shooting" and start thinking about "capturing."** Capture is "press record while you're already doing the thing." Shooting is "stop work, set up, perform, redo."

### The setup (one-time, ~10 min)

1. Phone clamp on a small tripod, parked permanently on/near your main work surfaces (desk, garage bench, kitchen counter). Not stored in a bag. Always out.
2. Phone always in vertical (9:16) mode for capture.
3. A note on your monitor: **"Press record before you start."**

### The rule

When you sit down to do *any* of your normal work — designing, formulating, woodworking, brewing — you press record first. You don't perform for the camera. You don't narrate. You just work.

Most of that footage is junk. That's fine. Once a week, scrub through, pull the 3-5 visually interesting moments, edit them into a 60s sequence with text overlays explaining what's happening. Voiceover optional.

### Editing software

Free options that work well for vertical short-form:
- **CapCut** (mobile + desktop) — most TikTok creators use this, has auto-captions, handles vertical natively. Free.
- **Adobe Premiere Pro** — you have Creative Cloud already. Heavier but you know the muscle.
- **DaVinci Resolve** — free, pro-level, steeper curve.

Recommend starting with CapCut for short-form specifically. Premiere if you go long-form later.

### The weekly editing block

One 60-90 minute editing session per week (Sunday evening?) produces 4 videos. Schedule them via Buffer / Later / native scheduler so you don't have to think about it the rest of the week. You're done.

## 5 — Equipment

### Buy now (under ~$250 total)

1. **Vertical-capable phone tripod with ball head**
   *Why:* Your current tripod only holds phone horizontally. You need 9:16 capture from day one.
   *Pick:* **SmallRig Mobile Vlog Tripod Kit** (~$50-70 on Amazon) — phone clamp that locks vertical or horizontal, mini tripod that extends to ~50cm, cold-shoe mount for adding a mic later. Or **Manfrotto PIXI Smart** if you want quality over price.
   *Skip:* Cheap Amazon-no-name tripods — phone clamps wobble, ball heads fail.

2. **Wireless lavalier mic**
   *Why:* Phone audio is the #1 thing that makes amateur video look amateur. Music covers a lot of sins; spoken audio covers nothing. The moment you do a voiceover, mic quality is the entire experience.
   *Pick:* **DJI Mic Mini** (~$170 for the 2-mic kit + receiver + charging case). Plugs into iPhone via USB-C, two transmitters means you can interview-style record or just have a backup. Excellent battery, tiny, magnetic clip-on.
   *Alternative:* **RØDE Wireless ME** (~$150) if you prefer RØDE's ecosystem.
   *Skip:* Wired lavaliers (tether you to the phone), shotgun mics on phone (poor for indoor close-up speech), USB desktop mics (won't help in the garage or kitchen).

### Buy in 1-2 months if you stick with it

3. **Single LED panel light**
   *Why:* Garage at night, kitchen with bad overheads — most of your environments will have lighting you can't control. One soft daylight-balanced panel = night-and-day difference.
   *Pick:* **Aputure Amaran 60d** (~$170) or budget pick **Neewer 660 Pro** (~$80) — both bi-color, daylight-balanced, soft when used with a small softbox.
   *Skip:* Ring lights (dated look, gives obvious "ring catch-light" in eyes/objects), dim panels under 30W (won't compete with overhead lights).

### Defer 6+ months (or maybe forever)

4. **Better camera (Sony ZV-E10 II / Sony FX30 / iPhone Pro upgrade)**
   *Why defer:* Modern phones (iPhone 13+, Pixel 6+) shoot 4K 60fps with computational stabilization that crushes consumer mirrorless cameras for short-form. The gap closes only at long-form, low-light, or shallow-DOF shots. You don't need any of that yet.
   *When to revisit:* If you commit to long-form YouTube and start hitting the ceiling of what your phone can do. Not before.

### Total commitment for now

Tripod + mic = **~$220**. That's it. Don't buy a camera. Don't buy lights yet. Don't buy a teleprompter, a fancy editing keyboard, a green screen, or a "creator desk." All of that is procrastination dressed as preparation.

### Storage — yes, you'll need more

Real numbers for the work you'll be doing:

| What | Size |
|------|------|
| iPhone 4K 30fps, 60 seconds | ~350-400 MB |
| iPhone 4K 60fps, 60 seconds | ~700 MB |
| Raw footage from a 90-min capture session (record-while-working) | ~15-25 GB |
| Edited 1080p export, 60 seconds | ~30-60 MB |
| One week of capture + edits + project files (4 videos pillar mix) | ~80-120 GB |

So a **3-month rolling buffer of raw + projects = ~1 TB**. You can live thinner than that if you delete raw footage right after publishing, but I don't recommend it for the first 6 months — you'll want to revisit clips, recut for long-form YouTube later, or pull stills.

**Tiered storage that handles this without overspending:**

1. **Working drive (fast, internal):** 2 TB NVMe SSD on the editing machine. **Samsung 990 Pro 2TB** (~$140) or **WD Black SN850X 2TB** (~$130). This holds the current month plus the previous month — anything actively being edited or might be re-cut.
2. **Archive drive (cheap, large, slow OK):** External 8 TB USB HDD for finished masters + the raw footage you want to keep long-term. **WD Elements / WD My Book 8TB** (~$140) or **Seagate One Touch 8TB** (~$140). Plug in once a month, copy completed projects over, unplug.
3. **Cloud backup (optional but smart):** **Backblaze Personal** ($9/month, unlimited backup of one machine including external drives) is the cheapest insurance. Set it and forget it. Worth it once you have ~50+ videos you'd hate to lose.

Total Year 1 cost: **~$280 one-time + $108/year for Backblaze**.

**Before you buy anything:**
- Uninstall games you're not actively playing — Steam library can be 200-500 GB easy. Steam → Library → right-click → Uninstall. Saves can sync to cloud.
- Move Steam library to a secondary drive (Steam → Settings → Storage → Add Drive) so games stop competing with editing storage.
- Empty Downloads, clear OneDrive's local cache, run Windows Storage Sense (Settings → System → Storage → Storage Sense).
- Free 100-300 GB this way before you order anything new.

**Don't buy:**
- A 4 TB internal SSD as your first move — overkill until you're actually shipping consistently for 3+ months.
- A NAS (Synology) — great long-term, but $400-600 with drives and adds complexity. Revisit at month 6 if archive drive shuffling annoys you.
- Cloud-first storage (Google Drive, Dropbox) for raw footage — too slow to scrub, too expensive at scale.

## 6 — First 5 video ideas (film these in May 2026)

These are starter ideas tuned to your existing work. Goal: ship one per week through the first month.

1. **"How I design a custom diecut template"** *(Studio process)*
   60s time-lapse of you working in your DME software on a folding-carton dieline. Title overlay: the job constraints (size, fold direction, paperboard caliper). End card: "25 years doing this. I run a studio now — sqrrlbrain.com."
   *Hook:* "This 4×6 box has 23 fold lines. Here's how I figure out which way they go."

2. **"Generating 100 designs for a t-shirt drop"** *(Tool & product launch — TRACT)*
   Screen-record + B-roll of you running the Imagen 4 batch. Show 3-4 final designs. End with the July 4 launch teaser.
   *Hook:* "I'm launching a print-on-demand brand for America's 250th. Here's how 100 designs get made in a weekend."

3. **"What's actually in your face cream"** *(Process journal — Crucible)*
   Voiceover only (mic quality matters!) over B-roll of you opening Crucible recipes on the laptop, ingredients on the kitchen counter. Pick one common ingredient (glycerin, niacinamide, whatever) and explain what it does. 90s.
   *Hook:* "I built a 706-recipe formulation library. Here's the one ingredient that's in nearly all of them."

4. **"AI-generated story engine, running on my phone"** *(Tool launch — Inkwell)*
   Screen capture of you playing a 60-second segment of Inkwell on your phone. Show a choice → outcome. End: "Free to play, you bring your own Gemini key. inkwell-story-engine.vercel.app."
   *Hook:* "Every choice you make rewrites the story. No two playthroughs match."

5. **"Building shelves for my kid's room"** *(Studio process — woodworking)*
   Time-lapse the cuts, sanding, finish. Reveal at the end. ~75 seconds.
   *Hook:* "Day job: industrial design templates. Night job: shelves for a 9-year-old."

Notice the pattern: every video is rooted in something you're already doing or have already done. Zero net-new work created for the camera.

## 7 — Weekly publishing cadence

**Target: 4 videos per week** (one per pillar slot: Studio×2, Launch×1, Journal×1).

This sounds like a lot. It's not, because of the capture-first workflow. The actual time per week is:
- Capture: 0 hours (it happens during normal work)
- Scrub footage: 30 min
- Edit + caption: 60 min
- Schedule + write captions: 30 min
- **Total: ~2 hours/week**

If 4/week feels like too much in month 1, drop to **2/week** (one process, one launch-or-journal). Better to hit 2/week consistently than miss 4/week and spiral.

### The publishing schedule

| Day | What |
|-----|------|
| Monday | Studio process — built last weekend, captured during the week |
| Wednesday | Launch / news from the studio |
| Friday | Journal — voiceover reflection |
| Sunday | Studio process — second one, often leftover from earlier week |

You schedule them all on Sunday night during the editing block. The rest of the week, you don't think about social.

### Cross-posting workflow

For each video:
1. Edit master in CapCut, export 9:16 1080p.
2. Upload to TikTok with caption + 3 hashtags (more on this in §8).
3. Upload same file to IG Reels.
4. Upload same file to YouTube Shorts.
5. (Eventually) Upload to Facebook Reels once Facebook is live.

Don't try to make different versions per platform. You're a solo studio — one video, four platforms, full stop.

## 8 — Hooks, captions, and not becoming "a content creator"

The dirty secret of editorial brands on social: you don't need to perform. You don't need to do the trending dance. You don't need to talk to camera. The brand just has to be **clear, consistent, and rooted in real work**. That's it.

### Hooks (first 3 seconds)

The hook is what stops the scroll. It must:
- Show motion or visual interest in frame 1
- State the *thing being made* or the *question being answered* in text overlay within 2 seconds
- Avoid "Hey guys!" or "Today I'm going to show you..." — these guarantee a swipe-away

Templates that work for studio content:
- "How a [X] gets made"
- "I built [X]. Here's why."
- "[Number] of [thing], one weekend."
- "This is the [tool/ingredient/step] that changes everything."

### Captions (the text under the video)

Short, declarative, no emojis. Editorial. The brand voice is the same as sqrrlbrain.com — confident, restrained, no exclamation points. End with one CTA when relevant: a link, a handle, a project name.

Bad: "OMG 🔥🔥🔥 you guys won't BELIEVE how this folding carton came together!! Drop a comment below 👇"
Good: "Folding carton dielines have to fold cleanly on the first try. This one took three iterations."

### Hashtags

3-5 per post, half general (#design #woodworking) and half specific (#dielinedesign #aiart #foldingcarton). Don't stack 30 hashtags — algorithms don't reward that anymore and it looks desperate.

### What you DON'T need to do

- Show your face
- Speak on camera (voiceover is fine; text-on-screen is fine)
- Have a "personality"
- Do trending sounds (some are fine occasionally; not your main strategy)
- Engage in comment-section drama
- Post stories or "BTS" daily
- Be online responding to DMs in real-time

You're a studio with a publishing cadence, not a person with a parasocial obligation.

## 9 — Metrics + when to pivot

Don't check analytics for the first 8 weeks. Seriously. The first ~30 videos are just learning what to make and how to edit it. Numbers are noise during that phase.

After 8 weeks (~32 videos shipped), look at:

| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Average completion rate (TikTok / Reels) | Whether your hooks work |
| Highest-completion video | What format/topic to do more of |
| Saves & shares (vs. likes) | Whether the content is *useful* not just *entertaining* — useful is what compounds |
| Follower growth slope | Trajectory, not absolute number |

**Don't optimize for views.** Optimize for completion rate and saves. Views are a vanity metric driven by the algorithm; completion + saves are signal that your content actually fits people.

### When to pivot

If after 12 weeks (~48 videos) you have:
- Average completion rate <30% → hooks aren't working, study other studio accounts and rework openings
- Lots of views but no follower growth → people watch but don't know who you are, add brand-frame opening + closing card
- One pillar dramatically outperforming others → kill the worst-performing pillar, replace with a sub-pillar of the winner
- Total radio silence (no one watching at all) → cross-platform issue, posting times, or hashtags. Diagnose before changing content.

## 10 — What NOT to do

Solo operators get pulled in many directions. Here's the do-not list:

- **Don't shoot vertically AND horizontally.** Vertical only until you do long-form YouTube.
- **Don't post AI-generated faces / talking-head AI clones.** It reads as inauthentic and torches editorial brand trust.
- **Don't outsource captions or scheduling to a "social media manager" yet.** You haven't found your voice. Wait until you have 100 videos under your belt.
- **Don't buy followers, engagement pods, or "growth hacks."** Algorithms catch this and tank your reach permanently.
- **Don't post about transitioning unless you choose to.** It's not your brand obligation. If you do choose to, it goes on a personal account, not the studio account, unless you're explicitly making it part of the studio's brand story.
- **Don't engage with negative comments.** Mute, block, or ignore. Never reply. Negative engagement boosts the comment in the algorithm.
- **Don't pause for "rebrands."** If you stop posting to "rethink your strategy," you'll never restart. Iterate live.
- **Don't compare yourself to creators with teams.** Casey Neistat had a producer, an editor, and a studio. You don't. Compare yourself to other solo studios.
