Brand guide

Brand Identity Design System Typography Self-as-Client

The brand wasn’t designed in a brand book and then deployed. The system was built by deploying it — palette tweaks, type adjustments, and glyph additions all merged through working code, on the live site, while real projects shipped through it.

This page is both the reference and the case study. Visitors who want the swatches and tokens find them; visitors who want the editorial story find that too. Because the website is the brand guide, every page on sqrrlbrain.com is also a working specimen. That feedback loop is part of the practice.

Color

The palette is built around brown ink on cream paper — the visual language of editorial print, deliberately oriented away from the agency-modern look. Vermilion is the consistent accent; warm-yellow is a recent tertiary added for selected emphasis; the brown family carries body and chrome.

--paper #faf6f0 Page background — warm cream, never bright white.
--warm #f2ebe0 Card / inset background. One tone deeper than paper for layered surfaces.
--accent #c8421a Vermilion. Italic emphasis, CTAs, eyebrows on dark grounds, the acorn glyph.
--warm-yellow #f4bc43 Tertiary accent. Reserved for highlight against vermilion or dark grounds. Never as primary text on cream.
--ink #3a1a00 Headings, body text. Deep brown reading as black at small sizes.
--brown-mid #7a4520 Secondary text, intro paragraphs, footer chrome.
--brown-light #b88c5a Eyebrows, metadata, captions. The text that frames content without competing with it.
--rule rgba(58,26,0,.1) Hairline dividers, card borders, swatch outlines. Calibrated to disappear at distance.

Typography

Three families. Each one earns its place by doing something the others can’t. The serif sets the editorial tone; the sans does the reading work; the mono carries the studio’s technical voice (eyebrows, metadata, captions, code-adjacent labels).

Lora · Serif · Display + italic emphasis

Selected work, structural thinking.

Used for H1s, page titles, italic phrases that get the vermilion treatment, and pull-quotes. Italic em with a vermilion fill is the studio’s signature emphasis pattern.

DM Sans · Sans · Body text

Body copy reads in DM Sans light. Carries paragraphs without claiming attention. The site is text-heavy on purpose; the sans has to disappear into reading.

DM Mono · Mono · Eyebrows + metadata

Letter-spaced uppercase for eyebrows, dates, file paths, hex codes, tag chips, and any moment the site needs to feel built rather than written. Reads as the technical undertone beneath the editorial top layer.

Custard Condensed · Display · Logo only

The wordmark is set in Custard Condensed and not used elsewhere. Reserving the typeface to the logo keeps the lockup recognizable; it doesn’t leak into headings or body.

Brand glyphs

Two custom marks. Both are intentionally small in their footprint — one structural, one rare. Together they let the brand carry personality without pressing on every page.

Acorn The brand glyph. A leaning silhouette with a scalloped cap, drawn at 11° off vertical so it reads as found rather than stamped. Inherits currentColor; default is vermilion.

Used as Inline period in italic phrases (notes index "asked" · about "dieline up" · homepage "worth keeping" · 404 "dieline"). Sized .3em of the surrounding type, baseline-aligned, so the lean of the silhouette harmonizes with the italic em. Also used as a centered end-of-post mark on long-form notes posts.

Don’t Don’t use it as a list bullet, navigation icon, or wallpaper pattern. Frequency is the whole point — the mark works because it’s rare.

Vitruvian Squirrel A polymath nod, owned as a punchline. Squirrel rendered in the pose of Leonardo da Vinci’s Vitruvian Man, against a warm-yellow disk inscribed in the brown circle and square. The visual carries the joke; copy never names DaVinci.

Used as The 404 page centerpiece — "you’ve wandered into an unmapped corner of the studio." Will surface again only when the studio’s cross-domain practice itself is the subject (a future essay header about polymath work; possibly a printed deliverable).

Don’t Don’t use as a hero image on regular pages, a footer flourish, or a brand watermark. The mark earns its weight from rarity. Once it’s on every page it’s wallpaper.

Voice

Editorial restraint with playful, optimistic, irreverent flourishes. The studio leans craft-focused and matter-of-fact — project copy describes what was built and why, without agency-speak or capability-deck language. When personality enters, it does so through visual choices (the acorn period, the squirrel) and small editorial moves (italic vermilion phrases, mono-laid-out metadata) rather than performative copy.

Do

Use plain, specific language. Name the actual constraint, the actual material, the actual decision. Trust the reader to follow technical detail; don’t pre-flatten it. Show breadth by naming domains where they apply (packaging + software + structural design + brand + IP), not by claiming polymathy directly.

Don’t

Don’t use AI-template phrases ("we believe," "we’re passionate about," "innovative solutions"). Don’t fabricate work or invent client brands to fill visual gaps. Don’t name DaVinci. Don’t pose. The studio’s edge is real production craft over 25 years; don’t pad with concept fluff.

The brand changes when the work changes — multi-disciplinary practice means the system has to host work that doesn’t fit a single category.

The current system: editorial restraint with playful flourishes, brown ink on cream paper, vermilion as consistent accent, warm-yellow as recent tertiary, an acorn glyph that quietly punctuates italic phrases, and a Vitruvian Squirrel reserved for the rare moment when breadth itself is the subject. Everything in this guide is alive, in working code, on the page you’re reading right now.
