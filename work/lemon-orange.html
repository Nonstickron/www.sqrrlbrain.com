Lemon & Orange Octagonal Box

Structural Design Dieline Full-Stack Faceted Geometry

One of the few pieces where I designed both the structure and the artwork. A pair of faceted corrugated boxes built to resemble the actual fruit at retail scale — one orange, one lemon, sister forms, both around 10″ tall. Full-stack from 3D model to printed dieline.

The shape problem

How do you turn a flat sheet of corrugated into the shape of an orange? You can’t curve corrugated. You can fold it. So instead of curving the surface, you approximate it: enough flat facets, joined at the right angles, and the eye reads the polyhedron as a sphere — or close enough that "fruit-shaped box" lands.

SketchUp first

The 3D plan came before the flat dieline. I built the polyhedron in SketchUp, working out how many facets the top crown needed (the rounded top of the fruit), how many in the middle band (the widest equator), and how the segments resolved at the base. Once the 3D shape held together visually, every panel angle and edge length came out of the model. The dieline was downstream — I unfolded the polyhedron flat once the geometry was right.

One dieline, two fruits

Both boxes are the same dieline. Same polyhedron, same facets, same construction — the lemon and the orange are structurally identical. The fruit identities come entirely from the print: different skin textures across the facets, different tones, different leaf shapes at the top crown, different brand language wrapping the body. One structural design carrying two products.

The print

I designed the artwork too. The skin texture across each facet is printed to read as actual citrus rind — pebbled, varied, the right hue depth for each fruit. Green leaves at the top complete the read. Type and brand language on the lemon ("LEMONS," the "WHEN LIFE GIVES YOU LEMONS" play across facets, the personal inscription) lean into the wordmark wrapping the polyhedron.

Inside

The orange opens. The white branching pattern inside isn’t a print — it’s the cut edges of the corrugated showing along the seams where the panels meet. The dieline geometry visible from the inside.
