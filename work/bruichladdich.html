Bruichladdich Decanter & Glassware Gift Box

Structural Design Dieline Packaging Crystal Protection

A high-end Bruichladdich gift box holding four crystal rocks glasses and a fluted crystal decanter. Produced for a partner company, so I wasn’t in the loop on distribution or end-customer details — just the structural problem. Which was the interesting part anyway: how do you protect five pieces of crystal in corrugated, no foam, with the box getting handled and shipped through a chain you don’t control.

The protection problem

Five pieces of crystal. Four rocks glasses, one fluted decanter. None of it goes in foam. The whole thing has to survive whatever the partner’s logistics chain does with it — handed off, stored, shipped, unpacked — and arrive presentation-ready.

Suspended construction

The crystal never touches the outside of the box. It’s suspended inside two layers of insert. The lower insert cradles each piece off the bottom of the box. The upper insert presses down over them. Crystal is sandwiched between the two layers and held in position by their geometry, not by adhesive or padding. Drop the box and the crystal doesn’t make contact with a wall.

The decanter

The decanter has flat sides that look like they should be the natural seating surface. They aren’t. Lay it flat-side-down and the proportions don’t fit the cavity; the insert wants to cradle the angles instead. Rotated, the decanter sits correctly, the proportions work, and the suspension holds.

The stopper is the other detail. It’s not secured to the decanter — gravity is what keeps it in the neck. Move the box horizontally and the stopper would come loose. The upper insert has a recess that presses down on the stopper through the lid, holding it in place across whatever orientations the box might see in transit.

The lid mechanism

The lid has side flaps that fold over the upper insert when the box closes. Those flaps press the upper insert down so it can’t lift, even if the box is jostled or held upside down. The whole sandwich locks the moment the lid closes. Nothing in the box can shift until somebody opens it.

Production

In-house at DME on E-flute. Wide-format print, Esko flatbed cut. Partner-company project — DME built it for another company that owned the Bruichladdich relationship — so my scope was the structural answer only. Artwork, brand assets, the printed lifestyle photo on the inside lid, and distribution all sat with the partner.
