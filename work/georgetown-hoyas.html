Georgetown Hoyas Booster Gift Package

Structural Design Dieline Packaging

End-of-season thank-you package for top-tier Georgetown Men’s Basketball boosters — a printed letter from Head Coach Ed Cooley on the inside lid, a custom-labeled wine bottle, and an engraved bottle opener. Handed out in person to donors, which let us drop the over-engineered closure logic that mailing would have demanded.

The presentation problem

The brief asked for a shoebox — basketball team, donor gift, the format reads correctly. Inside that shoebox: a wine bottle. Wine wants vertical. Shoeboxes don’t do vertical. Laying the bottle flat in the box would have been the obvious move, but flat-bottle-in-rectangle is the visual language of a packaged-goods carton, not a gift. The product would have read like inventory.

The angle

The bottle goes corner to corner, on a diagonal across the long axis of the box. The depth of the box is set by the bottle’s diameter; the angle is set by the bottle’s length. Once the diagonal is fixed, every panel angle in the insert is downstream of it — cradle walls, opener-recess walls, the false-bottom plane underneath. Adding angles to a corrugated structure compounds difficulty quickly; the math has to come out right or nothing tolerances correctly when it’s glued up.

A vertical product in a horizontal box reads like inventory. The diagonal turned it into presentation.

Three pieces, no glue

The insert is a three-piece system. A false bottom holds the bottle opener up flush with the lid line, so when the lid closes it presses the opener into its recess. The main insert sits on top of that, cradling the bottle on the diagonal and hiding the false bottom from view. A small inverted piece slips into the opener’s recess to keep the opener from sliding under the surrounding flaps. No wet adhesives, no tuck tabs — double-sided tape only, in the few places the structure needs to commit. Swing-top lid; no closure mechanism beyond it because the boxes were handed out in person, not shipped.

Built in-house

Printed on the Agfa wide-format and cut on the Esko flatbed. E-flute corrugated. The whole job stayed in the building, which means the prototyping cycle was hours instead of weeks — you can iterate the diagonal three times in an afternoon when the press and the cutter are both down the hall.
