SqrrledAway is meal planning built around how households actually cook — multiple cooks, kid plates, presence at the table, real ingredients in the pantry. Manual where you want it. AI when you don’t.

Web prototype · in development

What it is

A household command center, not a personal tracker

Most meal-planning apps were designed for one user, one set of preferences, one set of macros. SqrrledAway starts somewhere different. The household is the unit of planning. Two adults coordinating through a shared shopping list. Kids cooking under a parent’s supervision. Someone’s mom visiting and joining the table for a week. Allergies that vary by person. Recipes someone hated last time.

The app sees the whole picture. The plan adjusts. The shopping list reflects who’s actually eating that week. Reactions from past meals shape what gets suggested next.

AI shows up when you ask for it — generate a week, suggest substitutions, surface taste patterns. The rest of the time it’s a clean, fast, manual planner that doesn’t hallucinate ingredients you don’t have.

How it works

01

Plan together.

Assign who’s cooking, mark who’s eating, react to meals. Kid-chef nights show a supervisor avatar. The household sees the same plan; coordination happens in the app, not the group chat.

02

Real ingredients, real workflow.

Pantry tracks what’s actually on hand. Quick-audit mode reconciles the shelf in about thirty seconds. The shopping list shows what’s missing, with attribution when someone else checks an item off.

03

Manual where you want it. AI when you don’t.

Free tier is fully functional without AI. Premium adds plan generation, substitution suggestions, and household taste rollups. You stay in control of the recipes.

Pantry to plate

Recipes that see your shelf

The Pantry tab knows what’s in the fridge, freezer, and dry goods cabinet. The Recipes library reads from it — every recipe shows the ingredients you already have. “What can I make right now?” ranks recipes by pantry match, so dinner planning starts with what’s actually here. Imports come from a URL paste, a manual builder, or AI substitutions that respect your dietary flags.

After the cooking

Trends that add up

The Me tab keeps a record. Macro targets vs. what was actually eaten, week over week. Ingredient frequency. Eat streaks. Habit tracking for whatever you want to track — alcohol, exercise, sleep, screen time. Family member profiles capture allergies, diets, default serving sizes, favorites and dislikes. Reactions roll up over time so the planner sees who loves what and who’s been quietly skipping the broccoli.

Make it yours

One app, many kitchens

Twelve palettes — six light, six dark — plus three densities and ten typography pairings. Pick what reads right for your kitchen light, your eyes, your aesthetic. The High Contrast palettes flip a flag for thicker borders and bolder weights, designed for low-vision users who need the interface to stay legible at high zoom.
